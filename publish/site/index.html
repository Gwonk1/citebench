citebench: do AI models cite real law?

A citation-integrity benchmark. Each model gets the same legal research questions; every citation in every answer is checked mechanically against 10.7 million U.S. opinions. Runs dated 2026-09-22. Announced [date not set].

Conflict of interest. citebench is built by Graham Syfert, who runs syfert.com and the Syfert legal research tools. The same tools are one arm of the test, and the grader uses the same case-law database. Read the results with that in mind; the questions, grading rules and full reports are downloadable below so anyone can check them.

Three findings

  1. Frontier models almost never invent a case any more. They misquote real ones. Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra cited a nonexistent case 0.0% to 0.4% of the time (0 of 217 to 3 of 755 citations, 150 questions each). But 5.4% to 7.4% of their citations were misgrounded: a wrong case name, or a "quotation" that is not in the opinion. About 60% of those absent quotes (55 of 92) are accurate paraphrases put inside quotation marks. The rest are wording the court never wrote.
  2. With the Syfert tools, a 26B model on one desktop GPU and a mid-tier hosted model misquote less often than any frontier model does on its own. With the tools, every model tested misgrounded 1.9% to 5.7% of its citations; without tools, the models ranged from 5.4% (Opus 5.5) to 54.6% (Qwen 3.8 Flash). Gemma 4 26B with the tools: misgrounded 2.8% (8 of 289 citations, 150 questions), against 5.4% for the best frontier model without tools (Opus 5.5, 30 of 560). Claude Sonnet 5 with the tools: misgrounded 1.9% (5 of 265) and the expected case found in 92.7% of answers (51 of 55), against 3.6% (GPT-6, 3 of 83) and 78.2% (Opus, 43 of 55) for the best frontier results on the same 55 questions. Two limits: on invented cases these rows and the top frontier rows are all under 1% and cannot be ranked at this sample size, and Gemma with the tools finds the expected case about as often as Opus and Fable do bare (70.7% against 72.0% and 73.3%), not more often.
  3. A free case-law index has a blind window on the newest law, and the harness shows it. Recent decisions get their volume and page numbers from a private publisher, and free databases often lack them, so a real recent citation and an invented one both come back "not found." citebench matches the case name and year against the opinions before calling a citation fabricated. For Fable 5.1 that moved 11 of 755 citations (1.5%) from "cannot resolve" to "real." Both rates are in the table. Why this happens (Methodology section 4).

Charts

How to read this

Fabricated
Share of citations that do not match any real opinion, even after checking the case name and year against the database. The case does not exist, or the volume and page point nowhere.
Fabricated (strict)
Share of citations the reporter index cannot resolve by volume and page, before the name-and-year check. The gap between the two columns is real cases the free index has no page numbers for yet, plus any invented page attached to a real case name (a name match does not confirm the page). We show both so nobody has to trust the reconciliation. Methodology section 4 explains the gap.
Misgrounded
Citations to a real opinion whose claimed case name does not match the opinion at that citation, plus passages presented as quotations that are not in the cited opinion, divided by all citations. Quotes are counted one by one, so a citation with two invented quotes counts twice. It does not catch a real, correctly named case cited without quotation for something it does not say.
Paraphrase share
Of the absent quotations, the share that are accurate paraphrases put in quotation marks (at least 60% of the words appear in order in the opinion). The rest are wording the court did not write. A paraphrase in quotation marks is still a misquotation in a brief, so it stays in the misgrounded rate; this column says how bad the typical one is.
Why chart 2 has two bars per model
Absent quotations are counted per quotation, not per citation, so one citation can carry two bad quotes, and an invented citation can also carry an absent quote. The two counts cannot be stacked into one bar. For Gemma 4 26B and Qwen 3.8 Flash without tools, fabricated plus misgrounded comes to more than 100% of citations for this reason.
Red
Share of verified citations to cases flagged as overruled or abrogated by Syfertize treatment data.
Gold recall
Share of questions where the answer cites the case later courts actually cite for the proposition. Another real case can be a fine answer, so this is a lower bound on usefulness, not an error rate.
Abstained
Share of answers with no citation and an express statement of uncertainty. The prompt invites this. An honest "I don't know" is a good outcome, and this column shows whether a low error rate was bought by refusing.
Warned on bad law
On questions whose expected case has been overruled or questioned, the share of answers that say so.

Rates are pooled over citations, so an answer with ten citations weighs more than one with two. Models that write fewer citations (GPT-6 Astra, 217) have wider intervals than models that write many (Fable 5.1, 755).

Method in brief

  • Questions. 300 questions, each built from a statement of law that later courts cite a specific opinion for; that opinion is the expected ("gold") case. 150 Florida, 60 federal, 90 from ten other states. About 10% of gold cases are deliberately overruled or questioned. The results above use the first 150, which every bare run answered.
  • Prompt. Five templates, each asking for case name, reporter citation, court and year, and telling the model to say so rather than guess.
  • Grading. Every answer goes through check_brief, the Syfert citation checker, against 10.7 million U.S. opinions from CourtListener and the Caselaw Access Project. Grading is mechanical and repeatable; the rules are in grade.py.
  • Grader accuracy. Graham Syfert hand-checked a random sample of the grader's quotation and case-name verdicts (the misgrounded column). Under the current grader, "quote absent" verdicts were right 90.5% of the time (19 of 21, 95% Wilson interval 71 to 97%), "quote found" verdicts 95.2% (20 of 21), and name-mismatch verdicts 100% (6 of 6). Eight of the sampled items are being re-reviewed because the review page showed the wrong source opinion for them. The fabricated column has not had its own hand audit yet.
  • Not tested. Harvey, Legora, Lexis+ AI and Westlaw CoCounsel. We could not obtain access on terms allowing a published automated evaluation. The harness is public; any vendor can run it and submit results, which will be published marked as vendor-run.
  • Known weaknesses. The gold set and the grader share one corpus; the tools arm uses the grader's own checker, so the bare-versus-tools gap measures the value of this toolset as graded by this toolset. Recent opinions are text from court PDFs, where encoding artifacts can hide a real quotation. Hosted models change without notice; a row describes that model on 2026-09-22.

Reproduce it

The harness is plain Python (standard library plus requests). Repository: github.com/Gwonk1/citebench.

citebench/
  data/questions.jsonl     300 questions (schema in SCHEMA.md)
  data/build_questions.py  seeded, deterministic question builder
  run.py                   asks each model; resumable; logs every tool call
  grade.py                 sends each answer to check_brief; grading rules in its docstring
  reconcile.py             name-and-year match for citations the index cannot resolve
  report.py                writes the tables on this page
  models.json              model ids and per-token prices
  keys.env.example         copy to keys.env (never commit it)

Keys. OPENROUTER_API_KEY and/or ANTHROPIC_API_KEY for the models; SYFERT_MCP_TOKEN for the tools arm and for grading (a free token from syfert.com/mcp works); LOCAL_OPENAI_BASE for a local llama.cpp model.

One command (one model, one arm, the 150 headline questions, then grade and report):

python3 run.py --model claude-opus-5-5 --arm bare --limit 150 --confirm-spend \
  && python3 grade.py && python3 report.py --max-qid q0150

run.py prints a cost estimate and refuses paid calls without --confirm-spend. --max-usd caps spend mid-run. python3 run.py --dry-run --arm bare is a zero-network smoke test.

Cost. All runs in this study, including the excluded Gemini 3.8 Flash tools run, retries and answers past question 150, cost $42.69 in API fees, as recorded per answer by the harness. The local Gemma runs cost nothing beyond electricity.
